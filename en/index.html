Process

How I work

01

Discovery

I sit down with the stakeholders and users who live the problem before generating any solutions, looking for the constraints that will make or break it.

Catching a hard constraint here costs a conversation. Catching it after launch costs a rebuild.

02

Definition

I turn discovery into explicit rules and scope, mapping a flow's full logic before touching a screen.

Well-defined edges mean predictable behavior for users, and no mid-build scope creep for engineering.

03

Ideation & Prototyping

I sketch in Sketch, prototype fast with AI-assisted tools like Claude Code, and work through structure collaboratively in Miro.

Getting to something concrete quickly means feedback happens before a direction hardens — cheaper to change a flow than a shipped product.

04

Specification & Handoff

I document not just what the interface looks like, but the logic behind it — component states, exact behavior, and edge cases spelled out directly on the flow.

This is where I save the most engineering time: specs precise enough to build and verify without a round trip back to design.

05

Validation

I confirm the solution holds up — through usability testing when the context calls for it, or stakeholder and engineering review of underlying logic when correctness and edge-case coverage matter most.

Far cheaper to catch a broken edge case in review than in production.

06

Launch & Enablement

After development, I review the implemented UI against spec to catch drift, and plan and ship the help content users need to adopt the feature.

A feature only creates value if people know it exists and how to use it.

About

Background

I've spent the last 5+ years designing digital products for enterprise and operations environments, currently based remotely in Brazil. My work focuses on translating complex, data-intensive systems into interfaces that reduce operational friction, balancing usability, business logic, and technical constraints in equal measure.

I own the full product design lifecycle, from discovery and requirements definition through specification for engineering handoff and post-launch validation. This end-to-end involvement lets me maintain design integrity from concept through delivery, rather than handing off a design and losing visibility into how it ships.

Values

Clarity beats decoration

An interface should be usable before it's impressive.

Simplicity is a decision, not a compromise

Especially in data-heavy products, the job is to strip complexity out of the interface — not hide it and hope no one notices.

Aesthetics and usability aren't in competition

When they seem to conflict, it is usually because one of them was not designed with enough care.

Design isn't done at handoff

Precise specs and being able to build what I design keep decisions honest. I also review the interface after development to make sure it matches the spec and the quality holds up.
