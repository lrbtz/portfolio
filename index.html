Process

How I work

01

Discovery

I talk to the stakeholders and users who live the problem to solve doubts and define requirements. Interviews, alignment across teams.

A constraint caught here costs a conversation, not a rebuild.

02

Definition

I turn discovery into explicit rules and scope, mapping a flow's full logic.

Clear edges mean predictable behavior and no scope creep.

03

Ideation & Prototyping

I use Sketch or Figma when needed, but Claude Code is my main tool. It lets me build working prototypes that closely reflect the final product.

Early, concrete feedback is cheaper than changing a shipped product.

04

Specification & Handoff

I document the logic behind the interface: states, behavior, and edge cases, and state the functional and non-functional requirements, precise enough for engineering and QA to build against.

Saves engineering a round trip back to design.

05

Validation

I confirm the solution holds up through a logic review with stakeholders and engineering. After development, I run a UI review of the implementation, checking for UI issues and flow problems against the specs.

Cheaper to catch a broken edge case in review than in production.

06

Launch & Enablement

I write and launch the help content users need to adopt the feature.

A feature only creates value if people know how to use it.

About

Background

I've spent the last 5+ years designing digital products for enterprise and operations environments, currently based remotely in Brazil. My work focuses on translating complex, data-intensive systems into interfaces that reduce operational friction, balancing usability, business logic, and technical constraints in equal measure.

I've worked with international clients from a wide range of nationalities, and I've delivered three large projects at Venturus.

I own the full product design lifecycle, from discovery and requirements definition through specification for engineering handoff and post-launch validation. This end-to-end involvement lets me maintain design integrity from concept through delivery, rather than handing off a design and losing visibility into how it ships.

Values

Clarity beats decoration

An interface should be usable before it's impressive.

Simplicity is a decision, not a compromise

Especially in data-heavy products, the job is to strip complexity out of the interface, not hide it and hope no one notices.

Aesthetics and usability aren't in competition

When they seem to conflict, it is usually because one of them was not designed with enough care.

Design isn't done at handoff

Precise specs and being able to build what I design keep decisions honest. I also review the interface after development to make sure it matches the spec and the quality holds up.
